# Root-gloss register policy — Russian citation glosses for Sanskrit verb roots

_Created: 04-08-2026 · Last updated: 04-08-2026_

**Status:** adopted 04-08-2026 (H1860). Governs every *curated* Russian gloss attached to a
Sanskrit verb root (dhātu) in this repo and in gloss batches authored upstream in
[SanskritLexicography/RussianTranslation](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/tree/master/RussianTranslation).
Before this policy each batch re-decided register locally, so the corpus mixes
dictionary-neutral and pedagogical voices — measured in [§6](#6--measured-inconsistency-in-the-committed-data).

---

## 1 · Two layers — what this policy does and does NOT govern

| Layer | Example | Governed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive corpus rollup** — [root_glossary.tsv](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritRussian/blob/main/root_glossary.tsv) / [.jsonl](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritRussian/blob/main/root_glossary.jsonl), the ranked surface translations aggregated from the 1.09M-token corpus | √gam → «пришел (196) · отправился (177) · ушел (141)…» | **No** — this is *data*, generated by the pipeline; finite forms are what the translators wrote and stay as-is. Never retro-edit it by hand ([CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritRussian/blob/main/CLAUDE.md) § What not to touch). |
| **Citation gloss** — any *authored* headline gloss for a root: curated gloss batches, dictionary entries, UI headline lines, sample docs, teaching materials | √gam → «идти / пойти; отправляться» | **Yes** — everything below. |

A conversion sweep of existing curated surfaces is **separate work** (out of scope here, per
the H1860 fence); this file is the norm new batches follow and old ones converge to.

## 2 · Target register: dictionary-neutral, not pedagogical

**The citation gloss of a verb root is written in the dictionary-neutral register: Russian
infinitive(s), imperfective first.** Not the pedagogical/narrative voice (finite forms,
imperatives, converbs) that dominates the corpus rollup.

Why:

1. **The dhātu is unmarked.** A Sanskrit root carries no person, number, tense or mood; the
   only Russian verb form equally unmarked is the infinitive. Glossing √gam as «пришел»
   silently adds 3sg + past + perfective + masculine — four categories the root does not have.
2. **Russian lexicographic tradition.** Kochergina's Санскритско-русский словарь and every
   standard bilingual dictionary cite verbs by infinitive; matching it makes the glossary
   composable with existing reference works.
3. **The corpus voice is an artifact, not a choice.** Rollup glosses are finite because epic
   narrative is finite (hence the «пришел / сказал / убил» tide in §6) — reproducing that in a
   citation gloss imports narrative bias into lexicography.

## 3 · The verb-root rule: person · aspect · voice

**Person / number / tense / mood — none.** Citation glosses are infinitives only. Finite
forms («достигает», «расскажу»), imperatives («ступай», «слушай»), converbs («увидев»,
«придя») and participles («побуждаемый», «воспетый») are banned as citation glosses, whatever
their corpus rank.

**Aspect — imperfective head, perfective pair after «/» when lexicalized.**

- The head gloss is the imperfective infinitive: «делать», «идти», «убивать».
- When the Russian aspectual pair is load-bearing for the root's meaning, give it as
  «impf / pf»: «делать / сделать», «говорить / сказать». Omit the perfective when it adds
  nothing («стоять», «пребывать»).
- **Never perfective alone** — a bare perfective («сделать», «убить») smuggles in completed
  aspect the root does not encode.

**Voice — active infinitive by default.**

- Reflexive «-ся» only when the Russian equivalent is itself the lexical match:
  «становиться / стать» for √bhū 'become', «находиться» for √sthā 'be situated'. Never as a
  passive transform of an active gloss («убиваться» for √han is a defect).
- Passive sense of a root is expressed by the active infinitive of the Russian verb plus a
  labelled note if genuinely needed — never by a passive participle («убит», «повержен»,
  «сказано» are corpus voice, not citation voice).
- Causative stems rolled into a root (`-ay-` lemmas: `darSay`, `janay`, `vAray`) are glossed
  with the Russian causative-transitive verb: «показывать / показать», «порождать / породить»,
  «сдерживать, останавливать».

**Idiomatic-modal exception (declared, not silent).** A few roots lexicalize in Russian as a
modal or predicative, not a verb: √arh ≈ «должен» (modal). The citation gloss still leads
with an infinitive phrase — «заслуживать; быть должным» — and the modal usage goes in a
bracketed note: «(в сочетаниях: должен)». The exception must be visible in the gloss, never
an unmarked finite form.

**Nominal glosses on verb roots are defects.** A verb-root row whose gloss is a noun or
adjective («еда» on `ASay`, «решительный» on `sA`) is either a rollup artifact or a missing
verbal gloss — flag it, don't imitate it.

## 4 · Format conventions

- Lowercase, no trailing period.
- Synonym senses separated by «;» in falling corpus-frequency order; near-synonyms within a
  sense by «,»: «идти / пойти; отправляться».
- Aspect pair joined by « / » (impf first). Reflexive verbs cite with «-ся» attached.
- One gloss line per root ≤ ~60 characters; long sense inventories belong to the lemma layer,
  not the root headline.

## 6 · Measured inconsistency in the committed data

Register class of the **top-1** (highest-n) gloss per verb root, over the 1,205 verb roots /
17,303 verb rows in [root_glossary.tsv](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritRussian/blob/main/root_glossary.tsv)
(04-08-2026, suffix-heuristic classifier — see caveat):

| Top-1 gloss register class | roots | share |
|---|--:|--:|
| present/future finite | 385 | 32.0 % |
| past finite | 235 | 19.5 % |
| other / nominal | 231 | 19.2 % |
| converb-like | 156 | 12.9 % |
| imperative-like | 85 | 7.1 % |
| **infinitive (the target register)** | **70** | **5.8 %** |
| participle | 43 | 3.6 % |

Register mixing *within* a single root's top-10 glosses: **678 of 1,205 roots (56 %) mix ≥2
classes**; 319 roots mix ≥4 (e.g. `kf`: past + converb + imperative + present in its top 6).

**Caveat — heuristic, not parsed.** Classes were assigned by Russian suffix regexes on the
gloss's first word (‑ть/‑ти/‑чь → infinitive; ‑л/‑ла/‑ли → past; personal endings → present/
future; ‑в/‑я → converb; etc.). Ambiguous suffixes misfile individual items (e.g.
«приблизившись» lands in *other*, «благоволи» in *past*), so single-class counts are ±a few
percent — the headline finding (infinitives ≈ 6 %, mixing > 50 %) is robust to that noise.

## 7 · Known data artifacts (do not imitate, do not silently fix)

Found while sampling; these are **rollup/pipeline** issues, tracked here so gloss authors
don't propagate them:

- **Prefixed-lemma bleed:** `dA`'s top gloss «взяв» (n=169) is `ādā` 'take' rolled into the
  bare root — the longest-suffix root join is working as designed, but the citation gloss of
  `dA` must reflect 'give', not the prefixed lemma's sense.
- **Frozen-form glosses:** `pre` → «после смерти» (n=66), `prekz` → «при виде» (n=15) —
  prepositional phrases from idiomatic renderings; usable data, never citation glosses.
- **Nominal top-glosses on verb roots:** `ASay` → «еда», `sA` → «решительный», `Iq` →
  «достойный призывов» — 231 verb roots (19.2 %) lead with a non-verbal form.

## 8 · Where this applies

Linked from the two places gloss work happens:

- This repo's [README.md](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritRussian/blob/main/README.md)
  (the published glossary + UI headline glosses).
- The generation-pipeline home,
  [RussianTranslation/glossary/README.md](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/RussianTranslation/glossary/README.md),
  where gloss batches are authored and regenerated.

Any future curated root-gloss batch (LLM or human) cites this file in its brief and is
reviewed against §§2–4. Non-conforming *new* glosses are defects; non-conforming *existing*
corpus data is not (see §1).
